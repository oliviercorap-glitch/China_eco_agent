US Treasury yields post largest surge since 'liberation day' tariff shock

Reading
The FT headline indicates a sharp upward move in US Treasury yields, described as the biggest since the April tariff announcement episode that roiled global markets. The article body is inaccessible due to a security verification block, so the magnitude, tenor, and driver cannot be confirmed from the source. A yield spike of this character typically reflects either inflation repricing, fiscal supply concerns, or a repricing of Fed policy expectations.
Manufacturing impact
Higher US yields tighten global financial conditions and typically strengthen the dollar, which pressures the CNY and can widen the CNY/EUR cross. For TLD China, a weaker CNY versus EUR improves the cost competitiveness of Shanghai and Wuxi-built GSE exported to European and APAC markets, but raises the CNY cost of any EUR- or USD-denominated imported components and royalties. Higher global rates also raise the discount rate applied to TLD's own leasing book and customer financing.
GSE demand impact
Rising US yields feed into higher global leasing and aircraft financing costs, which can slow airline fleet commitments and, with a 12-18 month lag, temper GSE order flow. Conversely, if the move forces PBOC easing to defend domestic growth, cheaper CNY financing would support Chinese handler capex and TLD's leasing proposition in APAC.
