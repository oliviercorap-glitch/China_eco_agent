Executive summary
China's manufacturing sector is showing modest stabilisation with June PMI at 50.3, crossing the expansion threshold for the first time in recent months, supported by recovering export orders and a surge in infrastructure bond issuance to RMB 570bn. Input cost pressures are building across steel and aluminium, with HRC export prices up USD 8/tonne and aluminium hovering near RMB 24,800/tonne, adding approximately 2-4% to TLD's COGS for steel and aluminium-intensive GSE products. Lithium prices are softening to RMB 151,000/tonne, providing modest relief for TLD's electric GSE battery costs. The PBOC maintains a moderately loose monetary policy stance, though economists increasingly expect rates to remain on hold through 2026, limiting the anticipated boost to customer leasing demand. China's addition of 10 US entities to its export control list signals broadening trade tensions that require compliance vigilance but pose no immediate threat to GSE exports. The new Cybersecurity Law introduces compliance obligations with fines up to RMB 10m, requiring proactive gap assessment. Overall, TLD China faces manageable cost headwinds with stable demand fundamentals and policy support for infrastructure and electrification.

📈 GSE demand outlook (90-180 days)

2026 NEV subsidies shift to targeted support, up to RMB 20,000

China's 2026 new energy vehicle subsidy policy provides up to RMB 20,000 (12% of vehicle price) for scrappage-and-replacement purchases of NEVs, and up to RMB 15,000 for trade-in scenarios. Manufacturing impact
NEV subsidy policy supports the broader electric vehicle supply chain in China, including batteries and electric drivetrains that TLD uses in its electric GSE products. Continued policy support helps maintain scale economies in battery production, keeping costs competitive.
GSE demand impact
While subsidies target passenger vehicles, the policy signals continued government commitment to electrification. This supports the regulatory push toward electric GSE at airports, particularly as diesel bans expand. TLD's electric portfolio is well-positioned to benefit.
Recommended action

Important 🏦 MONETARY

PBOC signals flexible rate cuts and RRR cuts in 2026

Reading
People's Bank of China Governor Pan Gongsheng confirmed the central bank will continue a moderately loose monetary policy in 2026, flexibly using rate cuts and reserve requirement ratio reductions. Analysts expect up to two rate cuts this year. However, a June Bloomberg poll shows over half of 33 economists now expect rates on hold for the rest of 2026.
Manufacturing impact
Lower policy rates reduce TLD's financing costs for working capital and any RMB-denominated debt. RRR cuts improve liquidity in the banking system, reducing risk of payment delays from Chinese customers. The gap between policy rhetoric and economist expectations suggests uncertainty.
GSE demand impact
Rate cuts would lower leasing costs for TLD's GSE customers, supporting demand for financed equipment. However, if the PBOC holds rates steady as economists increasingly expect, the demand boost from cheaper financing may not materialise in H2 2026.
Recommended action
CFO should model both scenarios — rate cuts and rate hold — in Q4 leasing demand forecasts; maintain flexibility in financing arrangements to capture benefit if cuts occur.
