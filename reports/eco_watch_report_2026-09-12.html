Yen carry trade unwind risk could trigger broad EM currency volatility

Reading
The yen carry trade, which relies on borrowing cheap yen to fund higher-yielding assets abroad, is showing signs of unwinding as the Japanese currency strengthens. Analysts cited in the article suggest the strategy may be under pressure, though no specific JPY levels or percentage moves are quantified in the excerpt. This matters because carry trade unwinds historically trigger sharp risk-off episodes across global markets, including emerging market currencies.
Manufacturing impact
A carry trade unwind would likely strengthen the yen and weaken risk-sensitive currencies including CNY, which would be net positive for TLD's China export competitiveness in EUR terms. However, the same dynamic typically triggers commodity price volatility, particularly in copper and aluminium, which could disrupt TLD's input cost planning in Shanghai and Wuxi. Supply chain financing conditions for Chinese suppliers could also tighten if global liquidity contracts.

Conversely, a weaker CNY versus EUR makes TLD's China-built GSE more price-competitive for European and Middle East handlers evaluating fleet renewal.
