Executive summary
China's economy is slowing to 4.5% in 2026, missing the official 5% target, with property sector distress, trade tensions, and manufacturing consolidation creating a challenging operating environment for TLD's China operations. The US-EU coalition against Chinese industrial overcapacity and Section 301 tariffs of 10-12.5% pose direct risks to TLD's export competitiveness, while CNY appreciation of up to 5% could further compress margins on US-bound sales. On the positive side, falling lithium prices (down RMB 2,800-6,500/tonne) reduce electric GSE battery costs, and carbon policy reform creates a structural tailwind for electrification. Steel output cuts of 3.6% YoY and declining inventories signal potential input cost increases of 2-4% within 60-90 days. Priority actions include hedging USD receivables, locking in steel contracts, and renegotiating battery supplier pricing.
📉 Manufacturing margin outlook (30-90 days)
Manufacturing margins face a mixed but net-negative outlook for the next 30-90 days, with steel cost increases of 2-4% and CNY appreciation of up to 5% pressuring export margins, partially offset by 1-3% battery cost reductions on electric GSE. Section 301 tariffs of 10-12.5% on US-bound products represent the most immediate margin threat, potentially requiring price increases or cost absorption that could reduce US revenue by 10-15%.
📈 GSE demand outlook (90-180 days)
GSE demand outlook is cautiously positive for the next 90-180 days, supported by airport infrastructure investment in Qingdao and Sichuan, carbon policy-driven electrification mandates, and falling battery costs improving electric GSE economics. However, property sector distress and 4.5% GDP growth create headwinds for handler capex, with potential 6-12 month delays in local government-funded airport projects.

Important 📈 GDP

China GDP growth forecast to slow to 4.5% in 2026, below 5% target

Reading
Deloitte analysis projects China's economy slowing to 4.5% in 2026 from approximately 5% in 2025, driven by weak domestic demand, deep real estate adjustment, and trade friction. The report highlights "anti-involution" policies accelerating capacity consolidation and a shift from scale exports to supply chain integration.
Manufacturing impact
Slower GDP growth implies weaker domestic demand and potential overcapacity in manufacturing, which could keep steel and aluminium prices subdued but also intensify competitive pressure on TLD's pricing. The "anti-involution" campaign may accelerate industry consolidation, potentially benefiting larger, more efficient manufacturers like TLD.
GSE demand impact
A 0.5 percentage point GDP miss versus target signals reduced business confidence and potential deferral of airport infrastructure and handler capex. However, the shift toward supply chain integration and high-value manufacturing could support premium GSE demand from logistics and e-commerce-driven air cargo expansion.
Recommended action
CFO should stress-test 2027 revenue forecasts against a 4.5% GDP scenario and identify cost reduction levers; commercial team should focus on high-value electric and automated GSE segments less sensitive to broad GDP slowdown.

Watch 📋 POLICY

China shifts from energy consumption to carbon emission dual control

Reading
China's 2026 government work report replaced "energy consumption dual control" with "carbon emission dual control," targeting a 3.8% carbon intensity reduction in 2026 and 17% cumulative reduction under the 15th Five-Year Plan. Green electricity usage is no longer subject to rigid energy consumption caps.
Manufacturing impact
TLD's Shanghai and Wuxi plants can potentially increase production without hitting energy consumption ceilings if they procure green electricity, reducing regulatory risk and enabling capacity expansion. However, compliance with carbon accounting and reporting requirements will add administrative costs.
GSE demand impact
The carbon policy shift reinforces the electrification trend in aviation ground support, as airports and handlers face increasing pressure to reduce Scope 1 emissions from diesel GSE. This creates a structural tailwind for TLD's electric product portfolio over the 2026-2030 period.
Recommended action
Operations should evaluate green electricity procurement options for Shanghai and Wuxi plants within 60 days; commercial team should develop carbon savings messaging for electric GSE sales pitches.
