Important 🏦 MONETARY

China credit growth decelerates as M2 slows to 7.2% YoY in September

Reading
PBOC data for the first three quarters shows M2 balance growing 7.2% YoY, social financing stock up 7.1%, and RMB loan balance up 4.8% — all decelerating versus the prior month. Social financing incremental growth exceeded RMB 27 trillion, with direct financing (bonds and equities) rising as a share, reflecting a shift toward capital-market funding for "new quality productive forces" sectors. The slowdown in loan growth signals softer credit demand from the real economy.
Manufacturing impact
Slower loan growth (4.8% YoY) suggests tightening credit conditions for TLD's Chinese suppliers and customers, potentially raising working capital costs and delaying payments across the Yangtze Delta supply chain. The pivot toward direct financing may lower bond funding costs for large SOEs but leaves SMEs — many of TLD's component suppliers — more dependent on expensive bank credit.
GSE demand impact
Decelerating credit growth is a leading indicator of weaker capex appetite among airport operators and ground handlers, who rely on bank financing for fleet expansion. If loan growth continues to slow, GSE order pipelines for H1 2027 could face deferrals, particularly from smaller regional handlers.
Recommended action
CFO should stress-test TLD China's 90-day cash flow against a scenario of 4-5% loan growth and accelerate receivables collection from domestic GSE customers; monitor October credit data for further deceleration.
