Executive summary
China's economy is showing renewed weakness with July PMI falling to 49.2, the first contraction since March, and H1 GDP growth at 4.7% below the official target range midpoint. The manufacturing contraction signals near-term GSE demand risk as ground handlers defer capital expenditure, though 2025 air traffic growth of 5.5% with cargo surging 13.3% provides underlying demand support. Manufacturing input costs remain stable with steel prices flat and lithium prices showing potential for battery cost declines. Policy tailwinds from the 15th Five-Year carbon peak plan and NEV subsidy programs strengthen the case for electric GSE adoption. RMB appreciation trend and US-Japan FX intervention add currency uncertainty for export competitiveness. The overall picture is mixed: near-term demand softness from PMI contraction offset by structural electrification policy support and resilient aviation sector fundamentals.
