Executive summary
China's macro environment for TLD Group is a tale of two forces: favourable input costs and a strong airport infrastructure pipeline on one side, and deteriorating macro risks including property-sector weakness, local government debt constraints, and escalating trade tensions on the other. Steel prices at multi-year lows and lithium carbonate stabilised at low levels provide a 3-6% COGS tailwind and materially improve electric GSE economics, while the CNY's weakness versus EUR boosts export competitiveness by an estimated 2-5% on gross margins. Air traffic has surpassed pre-COVID levels and the airport pipeline across Chengdu, Guangzhou, Shenzhen, Xi'an, Kunming, and regional upgrades could generate several thousand GSE units over the next 24 months. However, the CRITICAL macro risk signal — driven by property distress, local government debt constraints, and US-EU tariff escalation — threatens both the infrastructure pipeline and TLD's export market access. Priority actions are to lock in low steel prices, accelerate the electric GSE commercial push, and stress-test the 2027 plan against a 15% infrastructure spending decline scenario.
📉 Manufacturing margin outlook (30-90 days)
Net manufacturing margin outlook for the next 30-90 days is positive, with steel at multi-year lows and aluminium range-bound providing a 3-6% COGS tailwind, partially offset by mild aluminium upward bias and imported component cost pressure from CNY weakness. The weaker CNY versus EUR adds a further 2-5% margin benefit on export revenue, making this a favourable window for TLD's China operations to build margin buffer against future macro headwinds.
📈 GSE demand outlook (90-180 days)
GSE demand outlook for the next 90-180 days is favourable, supported by domestic air traffic above pre-COVID levels, airline fleet rebuilds, and the multi-year airport infrastructure pipeline. The main risk is that local government debt constraints could delay regional airport upgrade funding, potentially reducing domestic GSE demand by 10-20% over 12 months, while trade tensions could dampen European and US demand for China-built GSE.

Watch 📊 PMI

China PMI stuck near 50 for third straight year, no threshold breach

Reading
The official NBS manufacturing PMI has oscillated between roughly 49.0 and 50.8 through 2024 and into 2026, never decisively breaking into expansion or contraction territory. The Caixin PMI runs slightly stronger, signalling that export-oriented and smaller manufacturers are outperforming the state-heavy segment. This is a sub-48/sub-52 no-trigger situation, so no CRITICAL flag applies.
Manufacturing impact
A flat PMI near 50 means TLD's Chinese factories face neither input cost spikes nor demand collapse from the domestic industrial cycle, keeping COGS broadly stable. However, sub-50 prints imply continued soft domestic order books for Chinese industrial customers, which could delay local supplier capacity expansions and keep lead times normal.
GSE demand impact
With PMI hovering at the boom-bust line, Chinese airport operators and ground handlers are unlikely to accelerate capex aggressively, but they are not in retrenchment either. GSE order flow from domestic Chinese customers should remain steady rather than surging, with export-oriented handlers in APAC more likely to drive incremental demand.
Recommended action
Maintain current production schedules and monitor the next two monthly PMI prints for a decisive break below 48 or above 52, which would require a demand or cost response within one week.
Important 🔩 STEEL

Chinese steel at multi-year lows on oversupply, tailwind for TLD COGS

Reading
Rebar and hot-rolled coil prices are near multi-year lows due to chronic steelmaker overcapacity and weak property-sector demand, with export volumes surging as mills offload surplus abroad. The downward pressure is structural rather than cyclical, meaning the cost relief is likely to persist through the next two quarters. No 10% single-month spike is indicated, so no CRITICAL threshold breach.
Manufacturing impact
Steel is a primary input for TLD's GSE structural frames, chassis, and platform assemblies, so multi-year low Chinese steel prices directly reduce COGS on China-built units. If steel constitutes roughly 25-35% of bill-of-materials for a typical tow tractor or loader, current pricing could deliver a 3-6% gross margin tailwind versus 2024 baseline. The risk is that export tariffs on Chinese steel from the US and EU could eventually disrupt the domestic pricing dynamic.
GSE demand impact
Cheaper steel lowers the cost basis for airport infrastructure projects in China, indirectly supporting the airport construction pipeline that drives GSE procurement. Lower input costs also allow TLD to price more competitively against European and US-based GSE manufacturers who face higher steel costs.
Recommended action
Lock in forward steel supply contracts at current multi-year low prices for the next two quarters to secure margin gains, and instruct procurement to renegotiate any legacy contracts priced above current spot.

Important 🔋 LITHIUM

Lithium carbonate stabilised at low levels, electric GSE economics improved

Reading
Lithium carbonate prices collapsed from 2022 peaks and have stabilised at much lower levels, driven by supply ramp-up and slower-than-expected EV demand growth in some segments. The stabilisation at low levels is a structural shift rather than a temporary dip, meaning battery cost reductions are likely to persist. No 20% single-month move is indicated, so no CRITICAL threshold breach.
Manufacturing impact
Lower lithium carbonate prices flow through to cheaper lithium-ion battery packs, which are the single largest cost component in TLD's electric GSE units. If battery packs represent 30-40% of an electric tow tractor's BOM, the lithium price collapse could reduce electric GSE unit costs by 8-15% versus 2022 levels, materially improving the payback period for customers switching from diesel.
GSE demand impact
Improved electric GSE economics directly support the business case for airport handlers to electrify their fleets, especially in markets with diesel bans or carbon reduction mandates. Chinese airport operators under the 14th Five-Year Plan's green aviation targets are prime targets for TLD's electric portfolio, and lower battery costs make TLD's pricing more competitive against diesel incumbents.
Recommended action
Accelerate commercial push of electric GSE portfolio in China and APAC, using updated lower battery cost data to refresh total-cost-of-ownership models for key handler accounts within the next 30 days.

Important 💱 FX

CNY weak vs EUR, boosts TLD export competitiveness but raises import costs

Reading
The CNY has generally weakened against the USD and EUR over the past two years, driven by China-US interest rate differentials, capital outflow pressures, and soft domestic growth. A weaker CNY makes Chinese-manufactured goods cheaper for European buyers but raises the cost of imported components and EUR-denominated liabilities. No 3% single-month move is indicated, so no CRITICAL threshold breach.
Manufacturing impact
A weaker CNY reduces the EUR-equivalent cost of TLD's China-manufactured GSE, improving gross margins on exports to Europe and other hard-currency markets by an estimated 2-5% depending on the magnitude of depreciation. However, any imported components or EUR-denominated royalties and management fees become more expensive in CNY terms, partially offsetting the export benefit. Net margin impact is likely positive for TLD's China operations given the export-heavy revenue mix.
GSE demand impact
A weaker CNY makes TLD's China-built GSE more price-competitive against European and US-based manufacturers in third markets, potentially unlocking orders in APAC, Middle East, and Africa where price sensitivity is high. European handlers may also accelerate China-sourced GSE procurement to capture the FX advantage, though this could be offset by any EU anti-dumping or tariff actions on Chinese machinery.
Recommended action
Review EUR-denominated revenue hedging strategy and consider increasing the hedge ratio on EUR receivables to lock in current favourable rates, while assessing whether import component costs warrant a dual-sourcing review.
