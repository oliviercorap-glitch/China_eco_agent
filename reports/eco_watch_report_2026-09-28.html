Executive summary
China's macro backdrop for TLD is broadly supportive on the cost and demand sides but carries two clear risks. The PBOC has confirmed a moderately loose stance with ample liquidity, special bond issuance has passed 70% of the RMB 4.4tn quota with front-loaded infrastructure spending, and the airport pipeline including Chengdu Tianfu, Guangzhou Baiyun T3 and Shenzhen Bao'an remains among the world's largest. Steel sits near multi-year lows and lithium has stabilised cheaply, both favourable for GSE COGS and electric product economics, while domestic air traffic is above pre-COVID levels and cargo is strong. Offsetting this, manufacturing PMI remains stuck near 50 with no expansion signal, the CNY continues to depreciate against the EUR, and the top six-month risks are property-sector drag, potential US and EU tariff escalation on Chinese GSE exports, and deflationary margin pressure. Net impact on TLD China is positive on cost and export competitiveness, neutral on volume until airport projects convert to orders, and exposed on trade policy. Priority actions are locking forward steel and rare-earth contracts, refreshing the electric GSE TCO model, and running a tariff exposure scenario.
📉 Manufacturing margin outlook (30-90 days)
Manufacturing margins should improve modestly over the next 30-90 days, driven by multi-year low steel prices, stabilised cheap lithium improving electric GSE economics, and a weaker CNY lowering the EUR cost of China-produced units. This is partially offset by mildly firm aluminium and continued rare-earth supply uncertainty, but no input threshold breach is present and the net direction is favourable.
📈 GSE demand outlook (90-180 days)
GSE demand over the next 90-180 days is supported by front-loaded special bond issuance, a deep airport construction pipeline and domestic air traffic above pre-COVID levels with strong cargo volumes. The main constraint is PMI stuck near 50, meaning handlers will prioritise replacement, retrofit and electrification over broad fleet expansion until airport projects convert to firm tenders.

Important 🌐 TRADE

US-China tariff cuts on basic goods lower input costs, rare earths unresolved

Reading
Washington and Beijing have agreed to slash tariffs on a basket of basic goods, easing landed costs on traded inputs, but rare earths were deliberately excluded from the published agreement. The exclusion preserves Chinese leverage over critical mineral supply while signalling a partial de-escalation in the broader trade conflict.
Manufacturing impact
Lower tariffs on basic goods reduce landed cost of imported components and semi-finished steel/aluminium articles TLD sources outside China, modestly improving COGS. However, rare earths remaining outside the deal keeps magnet and specialty motor input costs elevated and supply lead times uncertain for electric GSE drivetrains.
GSE demand impact
Reduced trade friction supports cross-border air cargo volumes, which underpins freighter utilisation and cargo-handling GSE demand at Chinese hubs. Any durable de-escalation also lowers the risk of retaliatory tariffs hitting Chinese-built GSE exported to US customers.
Recommended action
CFO to quantify tariff savings on the top 20 imported SKUs within 5 business days and instruct procurement to lock rare-earth magnet contracts for 2 quarters given continued exclusion.
Important 🏦 MONETARY

PBOC pledges flexible, ample liquidity; easing bias confirmed for Q4

Reading
The PBOC Monetary Policy Committee's Q3 meeting committed to using both incremental and existing policy tools, keeping liquidity ample and aligning money supply and social financing growth with economic and price targets. The language confirms a continued moderately loose stance with enhanced forward-looking flexibility.
Manufacturing impact
Ample liquidity keeps short-term funding costs low for TLD's Shanghai and Wuxi entities, reducing working capital and inventory financing costs. A weaker rate environment also supports the CNY at competitive levels, reinforcing export cost advantage from Chinese plants.
GSE demand impact
Cheaper system-wide financing lowers the cost of leasing structures for airport handlers and ground service companies, directly supporting TLD's leasing book growth in APAC. Easier credit also reduces the risk of delayed customer payments on large GSE capex orders.
Recommended action
Treasury to review the China leasing book's funding mix and price new lease offers against the lower rate curve within 10 days; sales to push leasing as a financing alternative to outright purchase.
Important 🏗️ INFRASTRUCTURE

Special bond issuance passes 70% of RMB 4.4tn quota, front-loaded stimulus

Reading
Local governments have issued over RMB 3.36tn of new special-purpose bonds year-to-date, exceeding 70% of the full-year RMB 4.4tn quota, with Guangdong, Shandong, Jiangsu, Zhejiang, Sichuan, Anhui and Henan leading. The front-loading signals accelerated infrastructure execution in the coming quarters.
Manufacturing impact
Accelerated infrastructure spending supports domestic steel demand at the margin, but with steel already in structural oversupply, the effect is likely to stabilise rather than inflate input prices. Regional construction activity in the Yangtze Delta supports logistics and labour availability around TLD's Shanghai and Wuxi plants.
GSE demand impact
A meaningful share of special bond proceeds funds transport infrastructure including airport expansion and regional aviation upgrades, feeding directly into the GSE procurement pipeline. Each new terminal or major airport upgrade typically generates 50-80 GSE units of demand, so front-loaded issuance is a leading indicator of order flow into 2027.
Recommended action
Commercial team to map which of the top seven issuing provinces have airport projects in the pipeline and open pre-tender dialogue with the relevant airport operators within 30 days.
Watch 📊 PMI

Manufacturing PMI stuck near 50, no threshold breach but no expansion

Reading
China's official NBS manufacturing PMI has oscillated between roughly 49.0 and 50.8 through the cycle, hovering around the boom-bust line with weak but non-collapsing factory activity. The Caixin PMI runs slightly stronger, reflecting export-oriented and smaller manufacturers outperforming the state-dominated segment.
Manufacturing impact
A sub-50 to low-50 reading means TLD's Chinese plants face neither input scarcity nor demand-driven cost inflation, keeping procurement markets soft and supplier pricing negotiable. It also implies continued slack in the Yangtze Delta labour market, limiting wage pressure on factory operating costs.
GSE demand impact
PMI below 52 means customer capex is not in broad expansion mode, so GSE order intake depends on airport-specific investment rather than general industrial confidence. Handlers are likely to defer discretionary fleet expansion and prioritise replacement and electrification retrofits.
Recommended action
Sales to shift emphasis from fleet expansion pitches to replacement and retrofit offers, and monitor the next NBS print for a break below 48 or above 52 within the week.
Watch 🔩 STEEL

Steel prices near multi-year lows on oversupply, favourable for GSE COGS

Reading
Chinese steel prices remain under persistent downward pressure from structural oversupply and weak property-sector demand, with rebar and hot-rolled coil near multi-year lows as mills cut output and surge exports to clear surplus. Iron ore prices remain correspondingly soft.
Manufacturing impact
Steel is a primary input for GSE chassis, platforms, stairs and structural frames, so multi-year low prices translate directly into favourable COGS for TLD's Shanghai and Wuxi plants. No threshold breach is present, but the direction of travel is a margin tailwind of several percentage points on steel-intensive product lines.
GSE demand impact
Cheap Chinese steel lowers the cost base of Chinese-built GSE relative to European and US competitors, strengthening TLD's export price competitiveness into APAC and Middle East tenders. It also pressures competitor pricing, so some of the input saving may need to be passed through to win volume.
Recommended action
Procurement to lock forward steel contracts at current low levels for 2-3 quarters to secure the margin benefit before any cyclical rebound, completing within 15 days.
Watch ⚙️ ALUMINIUM

Aluminium range-bound to mildly firm, no threshold breach, watch EV demand pull

Reading
SHFE aluminium prices are range-bound to mildly firm, supported by capped domestic smelting capacity and resilient EV and solar demand, while property weakness caps the upside. Lithium carbonate has stabilised at far lower levels after its 2022-2023 collapse, improving battery economics.
Manufacturing impact
Mildly firm aluminium is a modest headwind for TLD's aluminium-intensive GSE components, but the move is well inside the 10% threshold and manageable within existing cost assumptions. Stabilised low lithium prices materially improve the bill of materials for electric GSE, where battery packs are the single largest cost element.
GSE demand impact
Cheaper lithium improves the payback period on electric GSE, strengthening the commercial case for handlers to electrify fleets ahead of diesel restrictions. This supports TLD's electrification portfolio as a differentiator in APAC tenders where airports face emissions mandates.
Recommended action
Product and finance teams to refresh the electric GSE total-cost-of-ownership model with current lithium pricing and use it in Q4 tender submissions, within 20 days.

Airport construction pipeline among world's largest, Chengdu and Guangzhou lead

Civil aviation infrastructure investment continues to be prioritised within the transport capex envelope.

GSE demand impact
Each major terminal or runway project generates roughly 50-80 GSE units across ground handling, passenger boarding and cargo categories, so the named projects alone represent a multi-hundred-unit opportunity set. Regional airport upgrades add a long tail of smaller but higher-margin orders.

Important ✈️ AIR_TRAFFIC

Domestic traffic above pre-COVID, cargo strong on cross-border e-commerce

Reading
China's domestic passenger traffic has fully recovered to pre-COVID levels and continues to grow, while international recovery is slower but improving as visa-free policies expand. Air cargo is a bright spot, boosted by cross-border e-commerce volumes from Shein, Temu and Alibaba.
Manufacturing impact
Rising throughput supports airline and handler profitability, which in turn supports their ability to fund fleet renewal and GSE capex rather than deferring maintenance. Strong cargo volumes specifically drive demand for cargo-handling GSE, a segment where TLD has differentiated product.
GSE demand impact
Domestic traffic growth above 5% YoY is the classic trigger for handlers to refresh ageing fleets, creating replacement demand on top of new-terminal demand. Expanding visa-free travel accelerates international recovery, which will pull wide-body handling and international terminal GSE demand over the next 12-18 months.
Recommended action
Sales to prioritise cargo-handling and international-terminal GSE offers to the top five Chinese handlers, with proposals issued within 30 days to capture the current refresh window.
Important 💱 FX

CNY depreciation vs EUR continues, boosts export competitiveness but raises EUR import costs

Reading
The CNY has depreciated against the EUR over the past 12-18 months on divergent monetary policy, with PBOC easing against a holding ECB, weak Chinese domestic demand and capital flow dynamics. A weaker CNY makes Chinese-manufactured goods more competitive globally but raises the EUR cost of any imports into China.
Manufacturing impact
A weaker CNY lowers the EUR-denominated cost of GSE produced in Shanghai and Wuxi, directly improving TLD's group margin on units exported from China to Europe and APAC. Conversely, any EUR-denominated components or European-sourced subassemblies imported into China become more expensive in CNY terms, partially offsetting the benefit.
GSE demand impact
Improved export price competitiveness strengthens TLD's position in international GSE tenders against European and US manufacturers, particularly in price-sensitive APAC and Middle East markets. It also increases the CNY value of EUR-denominated leasing revenue, a translation tailwind for the group.
Recommended action
Treasury to quantify the net FX margin benefit on China-exported units and confirm hedging policy on EUR-denominated receivables within 10 days; no threshold breach yet but monitor for a 3% move in 30 days.
Important 📌 OTHER

Property drag, tariff escalation and deflation flagged as top six-month risks

Reading
The principal risks identified for industrial manufacturers over the next six months are persistent property-sector drag on local government finances and infrastructure execution, potential US and EU tariff escalation on Chinese exports including possible spillover to GSE and aviation equipment, and deflationary pressure squeezing industrial pricing power.
Manufacturing impact
Property weakness constrains local government fiscal capacity, which can delay airport project execution even where special bonds are issued, creating lumpy rather than smooth order flow for TLD's plants. Deflationary pressure limits TLD's ability to pass through any cost increases, compressing margins if input costs firm.
GSE demand impact
Tariff escalation risk on Chinese-built GSE and aviation equipment is the most direct threat, potentially closing or raising the cost of access to US and EU markets for units produced in Shanghai and Wuxi. Property-driven infrastructure delays could push airport GSE procurement timelines to the right by one to two quarters.
Recommended action
CFO and legal to run a tariff exposure scenario on China-exported GSE volumes to US and EU within 21 days and identify mitigation options including alternative assembly locations or tariff engineering.
