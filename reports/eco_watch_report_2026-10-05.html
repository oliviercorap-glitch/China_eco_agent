Executive summary
China's macro picture entering Q4 2026 is one of stable but fragile manufacturing conditions, with PMI hovering near the 50 line, steel prices low and favourable for TLD's COGS, aluminium range-bound at RMB 19,000-21,000/t, and lithium carbonate at RMB 120,000/t — above the 2024 trough but far below peak. The PBOC is signalling further RRR and rate cuts of 20-30bp across 2026, a clear tailwind for TLD's leasing model and customer financing, while EUR/CNY at 7.88-7.99 is modestly favourable for export competitiveness with no threshold breach. Offsetting these positives, China FDI has fallen to a 30-year low, the property crisis persists despite Evergrande's wind-down, and the DeepSeek risk brief flags deteriorating conditions including tariff escalation and deflationary pressure. The airport infrastructure pipeline remains a bright spot, with dozens of projects under the 14th Five-Year Plan implying hundreds of GSE units of demand, and domestic air traffic is above 2019 levels with cargo strong. Net impact on TLD China is cautiously positive on costs and leasing demand, but with material medium-term risk from tariffs, FDI retrenchment and property-sector drag on regional government capex.
📉 Manufacturing margin outlook (30-90 days)
Manufacturing margins for the next 30-90 days are stable to slightly positive, supported by low steel prices, range-bound aluminium and contained energy costs, with no threshold breaches in any key input. The main margin risk is deflationary pricing pressure limiting GSE price increases, and a stronger CNY eroding export margins by an estimated 1-2% if unhedged. Zero-carbon factory subsidies and green financing at 50-100bp below standard rates offer a modest offsetting benefit if TLD qualifies.
📈 GSE demand outlook (90-180 days)
GSE demand for the next 90-180 days is moderately positive, underpinned by domestic air traffic above 2019 levels, strong air cargo, and a multi-year airport construction pipeline including Guangzhou, Shenzhen, Xi'an, Chongqing and Kunming. PBOC easing lowers leasing costs and should stimulate handler fleet refresh, though PMI near 50 and weak FDI suggest customers will favour leasing over outright purchase. The key downside risk is property-sector drag delaying local-government-backed airport capex and tariff escalation disrupting export demand.
