Executive summary
China's economy is decelerating with 2026 GDP projected at 4.4% versus 5.0% in 2025, and July PMI falling back to 49.2 contraction territory after a brief spring rebound. The PBOC maintains moderately loose monetary policy with rate cut tools available, supporting financing conditions for both TLD and its customers. RMB has appreciated approximately 3% against USD since January, creating headwinds for TLD's export competitiveness from China. Government infrastructure spending through special bonds and ultra-long treasury bonds explicitly targets airport construction and equipment upgrades, providing a meaningful GSE demand pipeline. Property sector distress continues to constrain local government infrastructure budgets, while trade tensions with EU escalate as German manufacturers back tougher measures. Net impact on TLD China is mixed: domestic demand softening but infrastructure stimulus and electrification trends offer opportunities.
📉 Manufacturing margin outlook (30-90 days)
Manufacturing margins face moderate pressure from RMB appreciation against EUR reducing export competitiveness, partially offset by cooling factory-gate prices and potential solar energy cost savings. Input costs for steel and aluminium appear to be stabilizing after earlier increases, with June factory-gate prices recording first decline of the year. Net margin outlook for next 30-90 days is stable to slightly negative, with export margins most at risk.
📈 GSE demand outlook (90-180 days)
GSE demand outlook for next 90-180 days is cautiously positive, supported by government infrastructure bond financing for airports and equipment upgrade programs. Domestic PMI contraction suggests near-term order delays, but air cargo growth of 9.8% in East China and record trade volumes support cargo GSE demand. Electrification subsidies and diesel ban policies globally favor TLD's electric GSE portfolio.
