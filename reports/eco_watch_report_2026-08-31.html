Executive summary
China's manufacturing economy remains in a neutral-to-soft state with PMI hovering at 49.5–50.2, signaling muted capex appetite but no critical contraction. Input costs are favorable — steel prices are trending downward and lithium has collapsed, improving both traditional manufacturing margins and electric GSE economics. Air traffic has fully recovered with domestic volumes exceeding 2019 levels, creating genuine fleet refresh demand for ground handlers. Infrastructure investment in airports remains a policy priority with multiple CAAC-approved projects, representing a substantial GSE procurement pipeline. The primary risks are macro-level: prolonged property weakness, deflationary pressures, and potential US tariff escalation that could compress margins and dampen demand. Net impact for TLD China is moderately positive on costs, cautiously positive on demand, with the main threat being external trade policy shocks.
📉 Manufacturing margin outlook (30-90 days)
Manufacturing margins should improve modestly over the next 30–90 days, driven by lower steel and aluminium input costs and collapsed lithium prices for electric GSE batteries. Deflationary pressures and overcapacity could force price competition, partially offsetting input cost gains. Net margin outlook is positive, estimated at 1–2% gross margin improvement if current input prices hold.
📈 GSE demand outlook (90-180 days)
GSE demand outlook for the next 90–180 days is cautiously positive. Air traffic growth above 5% YoY and CAAC-approved airport projects support a solid procurement pipeline, but PMI below 52 and weak consumer confidence could delay order finalization. Expect order momentum to build in Q4 2026 as airport construction phases progress.

Important 📊 PMI

PMI hovers 49.5–50.2, flat growth signals muted capex appetite

Reading
China's official manufacturing PMI has been range-bound between 49.5 and 50.2 in recent months, indicating marginal contraction or flat growth. The Caixin PMI is slightly more positive, but soft domestic demand and export uncertainty keep overall momentum weak.
Manufacturing impact
Stable PMI suggests steady factory operating conditions at TLD's China plants with no sudden demand shock. Input costs remain manageable, but the weak momentum signals limited pricing power for finished goods.
GSE demand impact
PMI below 52 means customers remain cautious on capex; GSE orders will likely be deferred or scaled back. At 49.5–50.2, the reading is above the 48 critical threshold but below the 52 expansion trigger, keeping demand outlook neutral-to-soft.
Recommended action
Maintain conservative inventory levels for finished GSE units; prepare flexible production schedules to adjust to order timing shifts.
Important 🔩 STEEL

Steel prices trend downward, oversupply keeps input costs favorable

Reading
Chinese steel prices have trended downward in late 2024 and early 2025 due to oversupply and weak property sector demand. Government stimulus and infrastructure spending provide a floor, but excess capacity keeps prices subdued.
Manufacturing impact
Lower steel input costs directly reduce TLD's COGS for GSE frames and structural components. Assuming steel represents roughly 15–20% of manufacturing costs, a 5–8% price decline translates to approximately 1–1.5% gross margin improvement.
GSE demand impact
No direct demand signal; steel price weakness reflects broader industrial softness, consistent with the muted PMI reading. Infrastructure stimulus may eventually support airport construction, but timing remains uncertain.
Recommended action
Lock in current steel pricing with suppliers for Q4 2026 deliveries; consider extending contracts to hedge against any stimulus-driven price rebound.

GSE demand impact
