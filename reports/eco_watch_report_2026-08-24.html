Executive summary
China's macro environment for TLD Group presents a mixed picture with favorable input costs offset by currency headwinds and demand softness. Manufacturing PMI remains stable but subdued at 49.5–50.2, while steel prices have declined 10–15% YoY providing meaningful COGS relief for Shanghai and Wuxi operations. Infrastructure investment growing 5–7% with a strong airport pipeline, and air traffic fully recovered with double-digit cargo growth, support GSE demand fundamentals. However, CNY appreciation to 7.5–7.6 against EUR erodes export competitiveness by 3–4%, and deteriorating macro risks including deflation, tariff escalation threats, and local government debt constraints warrant caution. The RMB 20 trillion stimulus credit deployment provides a supportive liquidity backdrop for customer capex. Net impact is moderately positive for manufacturing margins but demand growth will require active pursuit of airport and cargo expansion opportunities.
📉 Manufacturing margin outlook (30-90 days)
Manufacturing margins should improve over the next 30–90 days driven by 10–15% lower steel costs and stable aluminium prices, with lithium's 50% decline enhancing electric GSE margins. CNY appreciation partially offsets these gains by increasing export prices in EUR terms, but net input cost relief is expected to deliver 2–4% COGS improvement on steel-intensive products.
📈 GSE demand outlook (90-180 days)

The airport construction pipeline in second- and third-tier cities plus cargo hub expansions should generate steady GSE procurement, though local government debt constraints and weak PMI may temper the pace of order flow.

Important 📋 POLICY

China expands fiscal-financial stimulus; RMB 20tn new credit deployed

Reading
Fiscal-financial coordination to boost domestic demand has shown initial multiplier effects, with 113 million residents and 6.22 million enterprises supported in seven months. Four investment and two consumption policies have driven over RMB 20 trillion in new credit, up RMB 880 billion or 4.5% year-on-year. The Ministry of Finance confirms further policy expansion in H2, including wider subsidy scope and higher limits.
Manufacturing impact
Broader credit availability and expanded subsidy programs support TLD's own financing costs and working capital access in China. The 4.5% credit growth signals liquidity is flowing into the real economy, which may ease payment cycles from customers and suppliers.
GSE demand impact
Stimulus targeting domestic consumption and investment supports air travel demand and airport infrastructure spending, both indirect drivers of GSE procurement. The policy multiplier effect suggests customer capex cycles may accelerate in coming quarters as credit conditions ease.
Recommended action
CFO should reassess working capital facilities in China within 30 days to capture improved credit conditions and negotiate better terms on RMB financing.
Watch 📊 PMI

China PMI stable at 49.5–50.2; subdued momentum persists

Reading
Official manufacturing PMI has hovered between 49.5 and 50.2 in recent months, indicating marginal contraction or flat growth. Caixin PMI is slightly more positive, but overall momentum remains subdued amid weak domestic demand and export softness. No threshold breach — PMI remains above the 48 critical level.
Manufacturing impact
Stable PMI suggests consistent but weak industrial activity, with no sudden demand shock for TLD's production inputs. The sub-50 reading indicates softness in the broader manufacturing sector, which may pressure supplier pricing and keep input costs manageable.
GSE demand impact
Muted PMI signals that customer capex in the aviation ground handling sector remains cautious. Airlines and handlers are unlikely to accelerate fleet refresh decisions while manufacturing confidence is flat, keeping GSE order flow steady but not robust.
Recommended action
Maintain current production levels; avoid inventory build-up. Review order book monthly and prepare flexible capacity adjustments if PMI drops below 49.
Important 🔩 STEEL

Chinese steel prices down 10–15% YoY; input costs favorable

Reading
Hot-rolled coil prices have declined 10–15% year-on-year due to oversupply and weak property sector demand. Production cuts and export quotas have partially stabilized the market, but ample capacity keeps input costs relatively low. No 30-day threshold breach flagged — the move is over a 12-month period.
Manufacturing impact
Steel is a primary input for GSE manufacturing; a 10–15% YoY decline directly improves COGS. For TLD's Shanghai and Wuxi operations, this translates to meaningful margin expansion on steel-intensive products like tow tractors, loaders, and container handlers, estimated at 2–4% COGS improvement depending on steel content.
GSE demand impact
Lower steel prices improve TLD's competitive pricing position versus non-Chinese competitors who face higher input costs. This supports export competitiveness in EUR and USD markets, potentially driving order volume growth.
Recommended action
Lock in current steel pricing with suppliers for Q4 2026 deliveries within 2 weeks to secure favorable input costs before potential market stabilization.
Watch ⚙️ ALUMINIUM

Aluminium stable at $2,200–2,400; lithium down 50% from peak

Reading
Aluminium prices remain range-bound at $2,200–2,400 per tonne, supported by supply constraints in China and moderate demand from EVs and solar. Lithium prices have fallen over 50% from 2023 peaks due to battery-grade oversupply but have recently stabilized at lower levels. No threshold breach — lithium decline is cumulative, not a 30-day move.
Manufacturing impact
Stable aluminium prices provide predictable input costs for TLD's aluminium-intensive products such as passenger stairs and cargo loading systems. The 50% lithium price decline improves battery costs for electric GSE production, enhancing margins on TLD's electrification portfolio.
GSE demand impact
Lower lithium prices reduce the cost premium of electric GSE versus diesel equipment, potentially accelerating customer adoption of electric units. This supports TLD's electrification strategy and may drive order volume for e-GSE products in the next 12–18 months.
Recommended action
Review electric GSE pricing strategy within 30 days to reflect improved battery cost position and capture market share in the electrification transition.

Important 💱 FX

CNY appreciates to 7.5–7.6 vs EUR; export competitiveness erodes

Reading
EUR/CNY has moved from around 7.8 to 7.5–7.6 over the past year, reflecting a weaker eurozone economy and relatively stable Chinese monetary policy. This represents approximately 3–4% CNY appreciation against the EUR over 12 months. No 30-day threshold breach flagged — the move is gradual.
Manufacturing impact
CNY appreciation reduces imported input costs for TLD's China operations, partially offsetting the export price disadvantage. Components sourced from Europe or USD-denominated raw materials become cheaper in CNY terms, providing some margin relief.
GSE demand impact
The 3–4% CNY appreciation makes TLD's Chinese-manufactured exports more expensive in EUR terms, potentially reducing price competitiveness in European markets. This is a headwind for export order volume, though partially mitigated by the steel cost advantage and TLD's brand position.
Recommended action
CFO should review EUR-denominated pricing for European customers within 30 days and consider hedging strategies to lock in current rates for Q4 2026 and Q1 2027 deliveries.
