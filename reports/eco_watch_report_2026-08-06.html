Executive summary
China's manufacturing economy has deteriorated sharply in Q3 2026, with the official PMI plunging to 49.2 from 50.3, breaching the boom-bust line for the first time in five months and triggering a CRITICAL alert. The contraction is driven by seasonal weakness, extreme weather, and continued housing market slump, offsetting the AI-driven high-tech export resilience seen earlier in the year. Input costs are mixed: steel prices are softening (RMB 3,220/tonne, down 1.2% in days) providing modest COGS relief, while lithium's 15.3% monthly crash to RMB 140,000/tonne significantly improves electric GSE economics. The RMB 295 billion advance infrastructure package and Dalian's RMB 50.9 billion artificial-island airport project provide demand-side opportunities, while the US-China trade truce offers temporary supply chain relief until November 2026. PBOC's confirmed easing bias supports cheaper financing for both TLD and its customers. Net impact: margin outlook is cautiously positive on input costs, but demand outlook is at risk from the PMI contraction.
📉 Manufacturing margin outlook (30-90 days)
Manufacturing margins should improve modestly over the next 30-90 days, driven by softening steel prices and the sharp lithium decline that reduces electric GSE battery costs by an estimated 2-4% on electric units. Aluminium remains elevated near USD 3,075/tonne, limiting margin expansion in lightweight components. Overall, input cost pressure is easing, supporting a 1-2% gross margin improvement if demand holds.
📈 GSE demand outlook (90-180 days)
GSE demand faces significant downside risk over the next 90-180 days, with the PMI contraction signaling customer capex deferral. However, the RMB 295 billion infrastructure package and Dalian airport project provide pockets of opportunity, while PBOC easing supports leasing demand. The net outlook is cautious, with order intake expected to slow unless policy stimulus accelerates.
