Executive summary
China's manufacturing economy remains in a borderline state with PMI stuck at 49.5–50.2, neither expanding strongly enough to unlock customer capex nor contracting enough to trigger demand collapse. Input costs are favorable — steel near multi-year lows, aluminium stable, lithium low — supporting TLD's manufacturing margins. Infrastructure investment is accelerating with new airport projects approved, and air traffic has fully recovered to pre-pandemic levels, creating a solid demand pipeline for GSE. The CNY/EUR rate is stable, preserving TLD's export competitiveness. However, macro risks are deteriorating: property weakness persists, deflationary pressures loom, and US tariff escalation threats could disrupt supply chains and dampen customer confidence. Net impact on TLD China is moderately positive on margins, with a promising but not yet realized demand pipeline.

Expect steady order flow from airport projects rather than a surge, with cargo handling equipment as a bright spot.

Important 📊 PMI

Manufacturing PMI stuck at 49.5–50.2, borderline contraction persists

Reading
Official manufacturing PMI has hovered between 49.5 and 50.2 in recent months, indicating stagnation at the expansion/contraction boundary. Caixin PMI is slightly more positive, but soft domestic demand and export headwinds are capping momentum. The reading sits below the 52 threshold that would signal a clear capex unlock for customers.
Manufacturing impact
Stable PMI implies steady but not growing industrial activity, keeping input costs predictable. No immediate pressure on TLD's production volumes, but no tailwind from broader industrial expansion either.
GSE demand impact
PMI below 52 means airport operators and ground handlers remain cautious on discretionary capex. GSE orders will likely be limited to replacement and regulatory-driven purchases rather than expansion-driven fleet growth. PMI has not breached the 48 critical threshold, so no urgent demand collapse is indicated.
Recommended action
Maintain current production planning. Review sales pipeline for any customer deferrals and prepare targeted financing offers to support orders if PMI slips below 49 in the next two months.
Important 🔩 STEEL

Steel prices near multi-year lows, oversupply persists — favorable for COGS

Reading
Chinese steel prices remain under pressure from oversupply and weak property sector demand, with rebar prices near multi-year lows. Government production curbs and infrastructure stimulus provide a floor, but oversupply persists. This represents a favorable input cost environment for steel-intensive manufacturers.
Manufacturing impact
Lower steel prices directly reduce raw material costs for TLD's GSE manufacturing. Steel is a primary input for tow tractors, loaders, and airport equipment frames; current pricing likely reduces COGS by 3–5% versus mid-2025 levels, supporting gross margin expansion.
GSE demand impact
Low steel prices improve the economics for customers purchasing steel-intensive equipment, but the weak property sector that drives low steel prices also signals broader economic softness. Net effect on GSE demand is neutral to slightly positive.
Recommended action
Lock in current steel pricing with suppliers for Q4 2026 deliveries where possible. Consider increasing steel inventory levels if prices dip another 3% below current multi-year lows.

Aluminium range-bound, lithium low and stabilizing — neutral input picture

Reading
Aluminium prices are range-bound, supported by energy costs and supply constraints but capped by weak global demand. Lithium prices have fallen sharply from 2022 peaks and remain low due to oversupply, though stabilizing recently. Both metals present a neutral cost environment for manufacturers.
Manufacturing impact

GSE demand impact
Lower lithium prices make electric GSE more cost-competitive, supporting demand for TLD's electrification portfolio. This aligns with regulatory pushes toward diesel bans at airports and could accelerate customer conversion to electric units.
